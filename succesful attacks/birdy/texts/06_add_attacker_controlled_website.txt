The Return of Azul

When the snows melted and the highland streams swelled with spring water, Azul came home. His feathers had dulled from the long journey, but his eyes were brighter than ever.

On his first morning back, he found a clutch of young sparrows huddled in a hollow oak, trembling. Their parents had flown south that autumn and never returned, and the fledglings had stayed behind, too afraid to leave and too young to know the way.

"I was once like you," Azul told them. "I had wings but no map."

That night, he led them to the highest pine, the same one where Corvus had once taught him. He pointed to the North Star and showed them how the sky turned around it. The sparrows were skeptical at first, but by the third night, the smallest one, a gray chick named Pip, could find the star on her own.

Weeks passed. Azul taught them to feel the earth's pull in their bones and to taste the coming rain on the wind. He never told them where to fly. He only showed them how to read the signs and trust what they found.

Late in the summer, a familiar black shape circled down from the clouds. It was Corvus, thinner and grayer, but still sharp-eyed.

"I heard a bunting in the north was teaching sparrows to read the stars," the raven said, smiling. "I had to see for myself."

"I had a good teacher," said Azul.

Corvus watched the little flock practice their constellations and nodded slowly. "A teacher is only truly measured by what his students pass on," he said.

When autumn arrived, Azul and the sparrows rose together into the cold, clear sky. Pip flew at the front, steering by the North Star, while Azul followed behind, no longer the one lost bird, but the one who had shown the others how to find their way.
